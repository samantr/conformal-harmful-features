# 4 Experimental Protocol

## 4.1 Datasets and outer partitions

The final robustness study used three multiclass tabular datasets from the UCI Machine Learning Repository: Dry Bean, Covertype, and Human Activity Recognition Using Smartphones (HAR). Source archives were pinned by SHA-256 digest and validated against the expected schema before analysis. Dry Bean contains 13,611 observations, 16 numeric features, and 7 classes. Covertype contains 581,012 observations, 54 features, and 7 classes; its feature representation comprises 10 quantitative variables, 4 wilderness-area indicators, and 40 soil-type indicators. HAR contains 10,299 activity windows, 561 engineered sensor features, 6 activity classes, and 30 participants. For HAR, the official release blocks were combined before the study-specific split so that the experimental partitioning, rather than the original release partition, determined train/tune/calibration/test membership.

Every seed used a four-way outer partition. Dry Bean and Covertype used class-stratified row-disjoint splits. Dry Bean was partitioned into 6,805 training, 2,268 tuning, 2,269 conformal-calibration, and 2,269 test observations. Covertype used 290,506 training, 96,835 tuning, 96,835 calibration, and 96,836 test observations. Because exhaustive feature selection is substantially more expensive on Covertype, selection was restricted to deterministic class-stratified subsamples of at most 20,000 rows from the outer training partition and 20,000 rows from the outer tuning partition. This compute budget affected only feature selection; final classifiers were refit on the complete outer training partition and evaluated on the complete outer tune/calibration/test partitions.

HAR used subject-disjoint splitting. In every seed, the 30 participants were allocated as 15 training subjects, 5 tuning subjects, 5 conformal-calibration subjects, and 5 test subjects. Candidate subject assignments were generated deterministically from the seed; assignments that failed to contain all six classes in every partition were rejected. Among valid assignments, the implementation selected the candidate minimizing the sum of row-count imbalance and class-proportion imbalance over 4,096 candidate permutations. A participant therefore occurred in exactly one outer partition within a seed. This design prevents direct subject leakage across partitions, but it does not establish exchangeability of activity windows across subjects or guarantee nominal conformal coverage under subject shift.

## 4.2 Classifiers and preprocessing

Two classifier families were prespecified: logistic regression and a small feed-forward neural network. For every fitted subset, standardization parameters were learned from the corresponding training data only. Covertype's one-hot wilderness and soil indicators were left on their original 0/1 scale rather than centered and rescaled.

Logistic regression used the `lbfgs` solver with \(C=1\). The iteration limit was 1,000 for Dry Bean and 500 for Covertype and HAR. The small neural network was a two-hidden-layer multilayer perceptron with 64 and 32 units, ReLU activations, \(10^{-4}\) weight decay, learning rate \(10^{-3}\), and early stopping with a 10% internal validation fraction. Dry Bean used batch size 128, a 200-epoch maximum, and patience 15. Covertype used batch size 256, a 60-epoch maximum, and patience 8; HAR used batch size 128, a 60-epoch maximum, and patience 8. Model randomization was seed-controlled and matched across feature-subset comparisons within a dataset/seed unit.

For the neural network, logits were reconstructed from the final affine layer instead of taking the logarithm of rounded `predict_proba` values. This retained information that could otherwise be lost when highly confident softmax outputs round to exact numerical boundaries. All temperature-scaled probabilities were computed with a max-shifted softmax.

## 4.3 Feature-selection comparators

The proposed one-shot and recursive conformal-harm paths were compared with the full-feature reference, matched random subsets, and five deterministic selectors: mutual information (MI), permutation importance, recursive feature elimination (RFE), SHAP, and conformal recursive feature elimination (CRFE). All selectors were restricted to outer training and/or outer tuning information; none used the final calibration or test partitions.

MI was computed with `mutual_info_classif` on training data. Permutation importance measured the tuning-accuracy decrease after permuting each feature, using 10 repetitions of the fitted full model. Standard RFE used a standardized logistic-regression selector and recursively eliminated coefficients until a complete ranking was obtained. Model-agnostic permutation SHAP used a training-data background and tuning-data evaluation sample under an explicit computation cap. CRFE used the multiclass beta-elimination rule with \(\lambda=0.5\): one-vs-rest linear SVMs were fit on training data, held-out tuning feature values contributed to the beta criterion, the largest-beta feature was eliminated, and the selector was refit recursively. RFE and CRFE used shared linear selectors, so their rankings did not depend on the downstream LR versus neural-network model; permutation importance and SHAP were downstream-model-specific.

Comparator subsets were matched to the proposed subset size within each seed. CRFE was therefore evaluated at the same retained-feature count as the proposed method rather than allowed to choose an independent stopping point. Ten random subsets were generated at each primary matched size as a descriptive reference. For the primary matched-selector inference, the proposed subset was compared separately with every prespecified deterministic selector rather than choosing a single best comparator from test performance.

## 4.4 Scaling and conformal grid

The primary operating point was Base APS at \(\alpha=0.10\) with a tuning accuracy-loss allowance of 0.01. Robustness evaluation expanded this setting over \(\alpha\in\{0.10,0.05\}\), scaling in {Base, TS, ConfTS}, APS, and RAPS. For RAPS, \(\lambda\in\{0.001,0.01,0.1\}\) and \(k_{\mathrm{reg}}\in\{1,3,5\}\), yielding one APS cell and nine RAPS cells for each alpha/scaling combination, or 60 cells in the complete grid.

All-feature models, tuning-selected primary proposed subsets, and deterministic comparator subsets at the primary matched sizes received the complete 60-cell grid. Other deterministic 1--5-removal sensitivity subsets received Base APS at both alpha levels, while repeated random subsets were restricted to primary Base APS at \(\alpha=0.10\). These scopes were fixed by method type and selection status rather than by observed test performance.

TS and ConfTS searched only the prespecified dataset-specific temperature grids. Dry Bean and Covertype used \(T\in\{1.0,1.1,1.2,1.4,1.6,1.8,2.0,2.5,3.0\}\). HAR used \(T\in\{0.4,0.5,0.6,0.7,0.8,0.9,1.0,1.1,1.2,1.4,1.6,2.0\}\). ConfTS used an equal split of its tuning input between threshold estimation and efficiency-gap loss evaluation. During tuning, candidate temperatures producing exact zero probabilities or exactly saturated maximum probabilities were rejected under the frozen real-data configurations. This tuning safeguard did not imply that held-out calibration or test probabilities would be free of numerical boundary events.

For every frozen scaling/score setting, a fresh randomized APS/RAPS threshold was estimated from the outer conformal-calibration partition. Final test sets were then formed using the same randomized-score construction and the paired test-uniform vector for that seed. No threshold or temperature was retuned after observing final coverage, set size, or numerical diagnostics.

## 4.5 Primary estimands and frozen sensitivity analyses

For each dataset, model, seed, and proposed path, the primary efficiency contrast was the all-feature mean prediction-set size minus the selected-subset mean size, so positive values favor removal. The primary accuracy contrast was selected-subset accuracy minus all-feature accuracy, again with positive values favoring removal. Empirical coverage and conditional-reliability changes were tracked alongside efficiency; a smaller set was not considered a valid conformal improvement merely because the size contrast was positive.

The primary subset was chosen using the Base-APS tuning path at \(\alpha=0.10\) and maximum allowed tuning accuracy loss 0.01. The frozen sensitivity analysis repeated the tuning-only subset choice under accuracy-loss allowances 0, 0.005, 0.01, and 0.02. A path step could be selected only when it satisfied the corresponding accuracy allowance, the fixed maximum coverage shortfall of 0.03, and a nonnegative tuning efficiency gain. Step zero was retained as the fallback. Independently, the diagnostic removal paths recorded 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5 removed features. These sensitivity cells were reported as a fixed grid; no test-time cell was selected as a new optimum.

Classification evaluation included accuracy, macro-F1, negative log-likelihood, and expected calibration error (ECE). ECE used 15 equal-width confidence bins. Conformal efficiency and validity metrics included empirical marginal coverage, mean/median/90th-percentile prediction-set size, and empty/full-set rates. Conditional reliability included size-stratified coverage violation (SSCV), class-coverage gap, and the maximum class-coverage deviation from the target. SSCV used set-size strata 0--1, 2--3, 4--10, 11--100, and 101--1000 truncated to the class count, with minimum stratum sizes of 25 for Dry Bean, 100 for Covertype, and 50 for HAR.

## 4.6 Pairing, grouped HAR evaluation, and statistical inference

All inferential method contrasts were paired by seed. Within a dataset/seed unit, methods shared the same outer split, selection-data indices, classifier seed for a model family, randomized APS/RAPS calibration uniforms, randomized test uniforms, and ConfTS split logic. These matched design choices were used to reduce irrelevant between-method variability and to make the seed, rather than individual result rows, the replication unit for primary inference.

For paired seed contrasts, the frozen reporting procedure computed the mean and median difference, sample standard deviation, standard error, a 95% Student-\(t\) confidence interval, Cohen's paired \(d_z\), matched rank-biserial correlation, an exact two-sided sign-flip test, and a Wilcoxon signed-rank sensitivity test. Exact sign-flip inference enumerated all sign assignments and was capped at 20 paired seeds, matching the maximum frozen seed count. P values were Holm-adjusted within the prespecified dataset/model/metric families. Nonsignificant accuracy contrasts were not treated as tests of noninferiority or equivalence.

Matched deterministic-selector comparisons were paired within dataset, model, seed, and proposed subset size. Each proposed path was compared against MI, permutation importance, RFE, SHAP, and CRFE individually; Holm correction was applied across the prespecified comparison family. Repeated random subsets were descriptive and were not treated as additional independent seeds.

Ranking robustness was also evaluated at the seed level. Complete conventional-selector rankings used pairwise Spearman correlation, top-1/3/5 Jaccard overlap, and Kuncheva stability across seed pairs. Proposed one-shot and recursive paths rank only the features actually removed, so their stability analysis used top-k Jaccard and Kuncheva measures without fabricating a complete-rank Spearman coefficient. Pairwise seed comparisons overlap and therefore serve as stability summaries rather than independent replications.

For HAR, final metrics were additionally calculated separately for each held-out subject. Each seed contributed five held-out subjects, and repeated windows from a subject were not treated as independent uncertainty units. Subject-level proposed-versus-all-feature size and coverage effects used the frozen 10,000-repetition two-way seed/subject pigeonhole bootstrap with random seed 810001. The bootstrap resampled the seed and subject dimensions with replacement and used percentile 95% intervals. This grouped analysis addresses repeatability of effects across the observed seed/subject structure; it does not by itself establish nominal conformal validity under cross-subject distribution shift.

## 4.7 Precision extension and final seed design

The robustness protocol began with the ten deterministic seeds 43--52 for all three datasets. A preregistered precision rule was evaluated after these ten paired seeds: a dataset was extended to 20 seeds when at least two co-primary size contrasts had 95% confidence-interval half-width larger than \(\max(0.01, 0.01\times\text{all-feature mean size})\). This rule was explicitly a precision criterion rather than a significance-rescue rule.

The ten-seed evaluation triggered the extension for Dry Bean and HAR but not for Covertype. The extension therefore added seeds 53--62 only for Dry Bean and HAR. The final design contained 20 paired seeds for Dry Bean, 10 for Covertype, and 20 for HAR. Twenty seeds was the preregistered maximum; no further seeds were permitted to rescue significance, coverage, or numerical behavior. The same scientific code, grid, split rules, models, selection procedure, and statistical definitions were retained in the extension.

## 4.8 Numerical diagnostics and execution policy

Probability diagnostics recorded exact-zero counts, exact-one maximum-probability counts, and mean maximum probability on held-out calibration and test data. The original Phase 8 execution guard treated any exact numerical boundary as a unit-level hard stop. After the benchmark revealed that boundary events themselves were an important robustness observation, an execution/reporting amendment replaced that hard stop with transparent retention while leaving the scientific design unchanged.

Under the amended execution rule, every prespecified cell and its originally tuned temperature were retained; raw boundary counts were preserved; additional boundary events relative to the matched Base condition were flagged; no temperature was retuned from final calibration or test data; no flagged scaling was replaced by Base; and no flagged row was deleted. Structural split, selection, grid, and checkpoint-provenance checks remained mandatory. Thus a completed unit denotes a complete, provenance-valid computation, not a declaration that every scaling condition is numerically safe.

The numerical flags are diagnostic frequencies over a dependent experimental grid. They are not independent failure probabilities at the observation, subject, seed, or model level, and co-occurrence with undercoverage is not interpreted causally. This policy preserves adverse numerical behavior as part of the robustness evidence rather than filtering it after outcomes are known.

## 4.9 Reproducibility and provenance

The scientific implementation used frozen commit `e4b3645`. Every dataset/seed unit recorded a split identifier, a selection-data identifier, the selected feature indices, selection seed, code version, and whether the classifier had been refit. Checkpoint manifests were bound to the dataset/seed unit, split and selection identifiers, model and scientific configuration, selected-subset digest, grid, and code version; resume was allowed only for a matching manifest. Source archives were reused only when their recorded checksums matched.

The execution code explicitly marked final subsets as frozen before outer conformal calibration and recorded that neither final calibration nor final test data were used for selection. Aggregation required the complete paired-seed design for the relevant dataset rather than treating partial runs as inferential evidence. Phase 7 results served only as frozen descriptive anchors and were not pooled as additional Phase 8 replicates.
