# 7 Conclusion

This study investigated whether feature removal can be guided directly by its effect on adaptive conformal prediction-set efficiency rather than by conventional predictive importance alone. We introduced an operational notion of conformal harm in which candidate feature removals are evaluated through retrained models using cross-fitted tuning-only evidence, subject to prespecified safeguards on classification-accuracy loss and empirical marginal-coverage shortfall. The selected subset is then frozen before fresh conformal calibration and held-out test evaluation. Both a fixed one-shot removal order and a recursively re-estimated path were studied, with the all-feature model retained as a valid no-removal option.

The empirical evidence supports the central idea, but only conditionally. H1 is supported with qualification: conformal-harm removals often differed from those produced by MI, permutation importance, RFE, SHAP, and CRFE, particularly for the small neural network, yet exact feature identities were not stable across seeds in all settings. The method should therefore be interpreted as learning context-dependent efficiency-oriented removals rather than discovering an intrinsic set of universally harmful features.

H2 is partially supported overall. On Dry Bean small NN, one-shot and recursive selection reduced primary Base-APS mean set size by 0.016968 (95% CI [0.007914, 0.026021]) and 0.017761 (95% CI [0.008777, 0.026745]), while mean classification accuracy increased by 0.003283 and 0.003680, respectively. Both paths also produced smaller matched-size sets than all five deterministic comparators after Holm correction. HAR small-NN selection showed mean set-size reductions of 0.017774 (95% CI [0.009855, 0.025693]) for one-shot and 0.013224 (95% CI [0.004643, 0.021805]) for recursive selection, but empirical coverage was 0.888214 with all features, 0.888247 after one-shot removal, and 0.892221 after recursive removal, all below the nominal 0.90 target. These HAR gains therefore represent set-size efficiency within the frozen evaluation, not a coverage-preserving conformal improvement.

H3 is also only partially supported. For HAR small NN, the one-shot residual feature gain under ConfTS was 0.008045 (supplied 95% CI [0.002255, 0.013836]) and the combined gain relative to all-feature Base was 0.023335 (supplied 95% CI [0.012116, 0.034554]). The additive excess was -0.009728 (supplied 95% CI [-0.015758, -0.003699]), showing sub-additivity rather than synergy; the selected ConfTS coverage was 0.887573. Feature removal and scaling should therefore be treated as distinct interventions whose joint efficiency and coverage behavior must be evaluated rather than assumed beneficial.

H4 is not generally supported. In the subject-weighted HAR analysis, small-NN mean-size reductions were 0.017361 (95% percentile CI [0.007472, 0.026864]) for one-shot and 0.013495 (95% CI [0.003330, 0.023091]) for recursive selection, whereas all four subject-weighted coverage-difference intervals crossed zero. Subject-disjoint splitting therefore reproduced the efficiency signal for the small neural network without establishing a corresponding reliability improvement or restoration of nominal conformal validity.

Taken together, the results show that feature contribution to adaptive conformal efficiency can differ meaningfully from conventional predictive importance, and that exploiting this difference can improve prediction-set efficiency in some model--dataset combinations. They also show why efficiency cannot be evaluated in isolation. A useful conformal feature-selection procedure must be judged jointly by set size, predictive performance, empirical marginal and conditional coverage, numerical behavior, and the stability of the selected features. Smaller prediction sets alone are not sufficient evidence of better conformal prediction.

The present conclusions are intentionally bounded by the experimental scope: three real datasets, two classifier families, a frozen APS/RAPS and scaling grid, and 20/10/20 paired seeds. Future work should test more diverse model classes and data regimes, develop feature-group or stability-aware formulations that distinguish interchangeable correlated variables from consistently harmful structures, and integrate procedures explicitly designed for grouped or shifted conformal validity. Within those limits, the study provides evidence that conformal efficiency is a legitimate feature-selection objective distinct from ordinary predictive importance, while also establishing that the value of such selection is model-dependent, coverage-sensitive, and inseparable from reliability diagnostics.
