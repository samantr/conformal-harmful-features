# 5 Results

## 5.1 Audit and evaluation population

The frozen Phase 8 evidence comprised 75,800 aggregate result rows from 50 dataset/seed units: 20 Dry Bean seeds (43–62; 31,100 rows), 10 Covertype seeds (43–52; 15,400 rows), and 20 Human Activity Recognition (HAR) seeds (43–62; 29,300 rows). Dry Bean and Covertype used stratified splits, whereas HAR used subject-disjoint splitting. The HAR subject-level audit additionally verified 146,500 subject-condition result rows against the aggregate results. All rows referenced scientific code `e4b3645`, and every dataset met the frozen final precision stopping rule (Table T1). Meeting this precision rule is an audit property only; it does not establish efficacy, statistical significance, numerical safety, or nominal conformal coverage.

The HAR subject audit reproduced all eight prespecified two-way seed/subject bootstrap summaries exactly and found five held-out subjects per condition, 20 seeds, and 26 unique held-out subject identifiers across seeds. The subject rows are therefore evidence for clustered subject-level summaries, not 146,500 independent subjects or windows. Original split-index files and individual unit completion manifests were not available, so the audit verifies recorded provenance and expected subject membership rather than reconstructing the entire historical split-generation process.

## 5.2 Classification importance versus conformal harm

The selected removals were generally distinct from those produced by conventional importance or feature-selection rules, supporting H1 with qualification (Table T3-H1; Figure F2). Within a seed, the HAR small-NN one-shot path had mean removed-set Jaccard equal to 0 against each of MI, permutation importance, RFE, SHAP, and CRFE (conditional on the 19 seeds with at least one removal). For the recursive HAR small-NN path, mean Jaccard was also 0 for four of the five comparators and 0.011111 for permutation importance. Dry Bean small-NN overlap was low but nonzero: one-shot Jaccard ranged from 0.005848 to 0.048956 across the five matched comparators, and recursive Jaccard ranged from 0.017544 to 0.101086. These quantities compare methods within the same seed and removed-feature count; zero-removal seeds are excluded only from this overlap estimand, not from primary efficacy analyses.

Distinctness did not imply stable feature identity across random seeds. The proposed small-NN top-1 across-seed Jaccard was 0.047368 on Dry Bean and 0 on both Covertype and HAR, based on pairwise seed comparisons (190 pairs for the 20-seed datasets and 45 for Covertype). In contrast, some LR paths were more stable at top-1, including 0.355556 on Covertype, 0.078947 on Dry Bean, and 0.089474 on HAR. These pairwise seed comparisons are overlapping and are not independent replicates. Figure F2 therefore separates within-seed cross-method overlap from across-seed identity stability. Because the proposed progressive paths are partial rankings, no complete-rank Spearman correlation is imputed to them. The evidence supports distinct selected removals, but not an intrinsic or seed-invariant set of uniquely “harmful” features.

## 5.3 Primary efficiency, accuracy and coverage

Under the primary Base-APS setting (alpha = 0.10; tuning accuracy-loss allowance = 0.01), the clearest efficiency gains occurred for the small neural network on Dry Bean and HAR (Table T2; Figure F1). On Dry Bean, mean set-size reduction relative to all features was 0.016968 for one-shot selection (95% paired t CI [0.007914, 0.026021], Holm-adjusted exact sign-flip p = 0.00000763) and 0.017761 for recursive selection (95% CI [0.008777, 0.026745], p = 0.00000763). These reductions were accompanied by higher mean classification accuracy: +0.003283 for one-shot (95% CI [0.001401, 0.005166], Holm p = 0.001648) and +0.003680 for recursive selection (95% CI [0.001882, 0.005479], Holm p = 0.000519). Observed coverage remained close to, but not uniformly at or above, the nominal 0.90 level: 0.899074 with all features, 0.899361 after one-shot removal, and 0.898744 after recursive removal.

For HAR small NN, one-shot removal reduced mean set size by 0.017774 (95% CI [0.009855, 0.025693], Holm p = 0.000717) and recursive removal by 0.013224 (95% CI [0.004643, 0.021805], Holm p = 0.004959). Mean accuracy changes were positive (+0.002660 and +0.003656), but their paired intervals crossed zero (one-shot [-0.001045, 0.006365]; recursive [-0.001336, 0.008648]); these results are not evidence of noninferiority. More importantly, HAR coverage was below nominal before and after feature removal: 0.888214 with all features, 0.888247 for one-shot, and 0.892221 for recursive selection. The HAR efficiency gains therefore cannot be described as maintaining 0.90 coverage.

The remaining strata were null or adverse in the primary comparison. Dry Bean LR slightly increased set size after selection: the one-shot size effect was -0.000992 (95% CI [-0.002455, 0.000471], Holm p = 0.184364) and the recursive effect was -0.001432 (95% CI [-0.002856, -0.000008], Holm p = 0.106918). The latter illustrates that the supplied unadjusted paired-t interval and Holm-adjusted exact sign-flip test need not yield identical decisions. Covertype effects were also not established: small-NN one-shot and recursive size reductions were 0.005704 (95% CI [-0.004183, 0.015592], Holm p = 0.457031) and 0.004272 (95% CI [-0.006037, 0.014581], p = 0.457031), while LR effects were 0.000377 (95% CI [-0.001443, 0.002197], p = 0.666016) and 0.001269 (95% CI [-0.000578, 0.003117], p = 0.312500). HAR LR likewise showed no established primary efficiency gain. Taken together, H2 is only partially supported overall, with the strongest coverage-qualified evidence on Dry Bean small NN.

## 5.4 Matched baselines and frozen sensitivities

Size-matched comparator results reinforced the Dry Bean small-NN finding but also exposed the limited generality of the advantage (Table T3-B; full effects in Supplementary Table T3a). For Dry Bean small NN, both proposed paths produced significantly smaller sets than each of the five prespecified matched selectors after Holm correction. One-shot gains versus MI, RFE, permutation importance, SHAP, and CRFE were 0.019744, 0.012252, 0.015469, 0.014610, and 0.019436, respectively; the largest Holm-adjusted sign-flip p value among these five comparisons was 0.000763. Recursive gains ranged from 0.014786 to 0.033715, and all five comparisons also survived Holm correction.

HAR small NN was less consistent. One-shot selection beat four of five matched selectors on set size after correction: MI (gain 0.016599, Holm p = 0.015541), permutation importance (0.016375, p = 0.000927), SHAP (0.022063, p = 0.000229), and CRFE (0.015930, p = 0.002350), whereas the RFE comparison did not survive correction (gain 0.011258, p = 0.070171). For recursive selection, only the SHAP comparison survived at the 0.05 level (gain 0.015908, p = 0.015541); MI, RFE, CRFE, and permutation comparisons each had Holm p = 0.070171. Thus HAR does not support superiority over every size-matched conventional selector, even before considering its undercoverage.

Other matched comparisons were heterogeneous rather than uniformly null. For example, Covertype LR recursive selection was smaller than matched CRFE by 0.016456 (Holm p = 0.019531), and Dry Bean LR recursive selection was smaller than matched MI by 0.017563 (Holm p = 0.002594), despite the absence of a reliable primary gain versus all features for those strata. Conversely, Covertype small-NN recursive point effects were negative against MI, RFE, permutation importance, and SHAP. These isolated comparator-specific results do not establish broad selector superiority.

The frozen sensitivity curves (Supplementary Figure F3 for small NN and Supplementary Figure S3 for LR) were interpreted as a prespecified grid rather than a search for the best test-time operating point. Across all four frozen accuracy-loss allowances, Dry Bean and HAR small-NN summaries retained positive Holm-significant size gains, whereas Covertype did not. Fixed removal-count effects were not uniformly favorable or monotonic. Because allowance rows, removal-count rows, score settings, and overlapping seed pairs share data, they are not additional independent replications.

## 5.5 Interaction with probability scaling

The scaling interaction was model- and dataset-dependent and supports H3 only as descriptive, sub-additive complementarity (Table T4; Figure F4). The clearest pattern occurred for HAR small NN. With all features, Base APS produced mean set size 1.021782; ordinary temperature scaling enlarged the sets to approximately 1.058356, whereas ConfTS reduced them to approximately 1.006493. The all-feature ConfTS scaling gain relative to Base was 0.015289 (supplied 95% CI [0.007005, 0.023574]), but its observed coverage was only 0.887779, so the smaller sets are not evidence of coverage-preserving scaling.

For HAR small-NN one-shot selection under ConfTS, the residual feature gain was 0.008045 (supplied 95% CI [0.002255, 0.013836]); the combined gain relative to all-feature Base was 0.023335 (supplied 95% CI [0.012116, 0.034554]). The additive excess, defined as B + C - A - D for all-Base (A), selected-Base (B), all-ConfTS (C), and selected-ConfTS (D), was -0.009728 (supplied 95% CI [-0.015758, -0.003699]). The negative excess means that the joint gain was smaller than the sum of the separate feature and scaling gains: the effects were complementary in the sense that a residual feature benefit remained under ConfTS, but sub-additive rather than synergistic. The selected ConfTS mean size was 0.998447 with coverage 0.887573; a mean size below one can coexist with empty prediction sets and is not automatically a successful operating point.

The recursive HAR small-NN path showed the same sub-additive direction, with additive excess -0.009405 (supplied 95% CI [-0.014915, -0.003894]), but its residual feature gain under ConfTS was 0.003819 with an interval crossing zero ([-0.002195, 0.009833]). On Dry Bean, ConfTS remained at T = 1 for the primary APS subsets, so it added no scaling benefit beyond Base in these comparisons. Covertype scaling effects were small or absent in several strata. H3 therefore does not support a general synergy claim. The H3 means were independently reconciled from frozen rows, but their interval-generation script is unavailable; the intervals in Table T4 remain supplied Phase 8 evidence rather than independently regenerated inference.

Numerical diagnostics place a further boundary on the HAR scaling results (Table T5b). Across the frozen dependent grid there were 10,006 raw-boundary flagged rows and 5,115 scaling-induced flagged rows. All scaling-induced flags occurred in HAR ConfTS, affecting 5,115 of 8,700 HAR-ConfTS grid rows (58.79%). Base HAR also contained 4,343 raw-boundary flagged rows, so the observed undercoverage cannot be attributed solely to ConfTS. These counts are dependent grid-row frequencies, not independent subject/model failure probabilities, and no flagged rows were removed from the analysis.

## 5.6 Reliability under subject shift

Subject-disjoint HAR analysis did not show a consistent reliability improvement, so H4 is not generally supported (Table T5; Figure F5). The exactly reproduced two-way seed/subject bootstrap showed small-NN subject-weighted mean-size reductions of 0.017361 for one-shot selection (95% percentile CI [0.007472, 0.026864]) and 0.013495 for recursive selection (95% CI [0.003330, 0.023091]). LR subject-weighted size effects were much smaller and their intervals crossed zero: 0.000810 [-0.003105, 0.004576] for one-shot and 0.001392 [-0.002821, 0.005612] for recursive selection.

Coverage effects did not provide corresponding evidence of improved reliability. All four subject-weighted coverage-difference intervals crossed zero: LR one-shot 0.000988 (95% CI [-0.001609, 0.003545]), LR recursive -0.000176 ([-0.002894, 0.002636]), small-NN one-shot -0.000012 ([-0.005227, 0.004836]), and small-NN recursive 0.003886 ([-0.003692, 0.010944]). Crossing zero is an absence of evidence for a directional coverage effect, not evidence of equivalence. Aggregate SSCV and class-coverage-gap changes were likewise mixed; for example, HAR small-NN recursive SSCV improved from 0.074235 to 0.066815, whereas the one-shot SSCV changed slightly adversely to 0.074446 and its class-coverage gap increased from 0.145819 to 0.151200.

Each bootstrap contrast contained 100 observed seed/subject cells formed from 20 seeds and 26 unique held-out subject identifiers, and used 10,000 two-way bootstrap repetitions over seeds and subjects. Figure F5 shows those clustered subject-cell distributions together with the frozen bootstrap intervals and the nominal 0.90 reference. Subject-disjoint splitting removes direct subject leakage, but it does not by itself guarantee exchangeability of windows across subjects or restore nominal coverage. The central reliability conclusion is therefore negative: efficiency gains for the HAR small NN were reproducible at the subject-weighted level, but reliability gains were not.
